What Is Brand Voice and How Do You Define Yours?

Brand voice is the personality that shows up every time your brand writes something. Here is how to define it, document it, and make sure your team actually uses it.

Brand voice is not your tagline. It is not your mission statement. It is the consistent personality that shows up in every piece of copy your brand produces, from your homepage headline to your email subject lines to your invoice payment reminders. When it is consistent, people start to recognise your brand by how it sounds before they see who sent it.

Start with three to five adjectives

The fastest way to define voice is to choose three to five adjectives that describe how your brand sounds. Not what it does or who it serves, but how it communicates. Direct. Warm. Knowledgeable. Irreverent. Confident. These adjectives become the filter every piece of copy runs through before it goes out.

Define what you are not

For every adjective you choose, identify its unwanted cousin. Direct but not blunt. Warm but not sycophantic. Knowledgeable but not condescending. Confident but not arrogant. The pairs are more useful than the single words because they prevent teams from overcorrecting in the wrong direction.

The difference between a brand with voice and one without it is the difference between a company and a personality. People trust personalities.

Write examples, not just rules

Show what on-brand copy looks like alongside off-brand alternatives. Take the same piece of information and write it three ways: one that sounds like your brand, one that is too formal, and one that is too casual. The contrast teaches the tone more effectively than any descriptor.

The test

Cover the logo on a piece of your copy. Could it have come from any other brand in your category? If yes, the voice is not distinctive enough. Your brand should sound like something specific, not something generic. That distinctiveness is worth building deliberately.
