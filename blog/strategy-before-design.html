Strategy Before Design:
A Case Study

A behind-the-scenes look at how we approached a recent rebrand. What we learned in the strategy phase — and how it shaped every design decision that followed.

When a logistics company came to us asking for a rebrand, the brief was simple: "We need a new logo. Something more professional." We said yes — and then we said we'd need four weeks before we touched a design file.

That four weeks changed the entire project.

What we found in the strategy phase

The company had been operating for three years. They had clients. They were growing. But when we asked them to describe what made them different from their competitors, they talked about their trucks. Their technology. Their routes.

No one could tell us what they stood for. Not the CEO. Not the sales team. Not the operations lead. They were a good business without a brand position — which meant every sales conversation started from scratch, and every marketing effort was disconnected from every other.

They were a good business without a brand position — which meant every sales conversation started from scratch.

The positioning work

We spent two weeks in discovery. Stakeholder interviews, competitor analysis, customer conversations. What emerged was something the team already knew but had never articulated: they were the logistics company that treated enterprise clients like partners, not accounts. Everyone else competed on price and speed. They competed on relationship and reliability.

That became the strategic anchor. Every design decision that followed was a visual expression of that position.

What changed in design

The logo we would have made on day one — based on the brief alone — would have been clean, competent, forgettable. The logo we made after four weeks of strategy was deliberate. The mark referenced precision and motion in a way that felt specific to what the company actually stood for. The colour palette was warmer than the category expected, because warmth expressed partnership.

Post-launch, inbound enquiries went up 40% in three months. Not because the logo was beautiful — because the whole system was coherent.

Strategy doesn't add time to a branding project. It removes time — because every decision after it gets made faster. The hours spent on positioning saved weeks in revisions.
