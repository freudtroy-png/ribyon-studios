Building a Global Brand
from Nairobi

What it means to build a brand from East Africa — and why geography is an asset, not a limitation, when you have conviction and the internet.

There is a conversation that happens every few months in the African startup and creative ecosystem. Someone builds something genuinely good — a product, a studio, a brand — and then hedges it. They make it sound neutral. They strip out anything that might signal where it came from. They make it safe.

The logic is understandable: if it could be from anywhere, it might appeal to everyone. The problem is that brands which could be from anywhere tend to feel like they're from nowhere.

The origin myth of global brands

Nike is from Oregon. Aesop is from Melbourne. Supreme is from downtown Manhattan. Patagonia is from Ventura, California. The geography isn't incidental — it's part of what makes each of them specific. Specificity is what makes people trust something. And trust is what makes a brand global.

The path to global relevance doesn't run through generic. It runs through deeply specific, executed with conviction.

What Nairobi actually is

Nairobi is one of the most interesting cities in the world to build a brand from right now. It's a technology hub, a design culture in formation, a market that demands real quality because consumers here are sophisticated and underserved. The brands that will matter in ten years are being built here today.

That's not a consolation prize for not being in New York. That's an opportunity. The founders who understand this — who build brands that are unapologetically from here while being technically world-class — are the ones whose companies will travel.

What to do with it

Build with conviction. Don't soften your origin story — make it part of your brand story. Choose design that reflects where you actually are and what you actually stand for. Work with people who understand the market you're building for, not just the market you're trying to impress.

The internet has made geography a choice, not a constraint. Choose wisely — and choose specifically.
