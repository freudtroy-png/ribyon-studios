Consistency Without Boredom:
A Content Framework

How to build a social media presence that's recognisable without being repetitive. A system for varied, on-brand content at scale.

The two most common content problems are opposites. The first: posting the same type of content so consistently that your audience stops paying attention. The second: posting whatever feels relevant that day, with no thread connecting any of it. Both are consistency problems. They just fail differently.

The solution isn't to post more. It's to build a system that makes consistent variation possible.

The content category framework

A content category is a defined type of post with a defined purpose. When you have four to six of them, you have a rotation. Rotation gives you variety without randomness — every post fits the brand even when no two posts look the same.

A simple framework for most brands:

Expertise — what you know. Insights, frameworks, opinions on your industry. Builds credibility.
Process — how you work. Behind-the-scenes, making-of, methodology. Builds trust.
Results — what you've achieved. Case studies, metrics, transformations. Builds proof.
Perspective — what you believe. Takes, opinions, positions on things that matter to your audience. Builds affinity.
Culture — who you are. Team, values, the human side. Builds connection.

Rotation gives you variety without randomness — every post fits the brand even when no two posts look the same.

The design layer

Once you have categories, the design system becomes simple. Each category gets a visual treatment — a colour, a layout, a typographic style. When a viewer sees your content, they recognise the brand before they read the copy. That's what brand recognition actually looks like in a feed.

The maintenance layer

A content system doesn't run itself. But it dramatically reduces the cognitive load of content creation. Instead of "what should I post today?" the question becomes "which category is it, and what do I have to say in it?" That's a much easier brief. The answer comes faster, and it's more likely to be good.

Consistency isn't about posting the same thing over and over. It's about showing up in a way that's recognisable — even when what you're saying changes. Build the system once. Use it forever.
